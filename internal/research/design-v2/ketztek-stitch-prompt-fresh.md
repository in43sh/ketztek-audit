# Stitch prompt: ketztek homepage from a blank page

Date: 2026-11-21. This replaces [the first Stitch prompt](ketztek-stitch-prompt.md) for new runs. It keeps the facts, honesty rules, accessibility floor and local-market findings from the [visual research](ketztek-visual-research.md). It drops every visual decision made so far.

## Why the first two runs looked like the old design

The two Stitch projects produced six concepts: Swiss Industrial Grid, Editorial Monograph, High-Contrast Workshop, Shift Handover Board, Technical Calibration Sheet, and Plant Floor Plan. All six belong to one family: paper, documents and technical drawings on a white or grey page, in black and yellow. The earlier designs (Rapport, Drawing sheet, Measured, options A to D) come from the same family.

The old prompt caused this:

- Its "starting points" were all documents: a handover board, a maintenance tag, a calibration certificate, a floor plan and a site diary.
- It locked the palette to black and yellow and banned every other hue that would give a different mood.
- It banned photographs, so the only pictures left were drawings of documents.
- Its verbatim copy came in the old section order (problem, four steps, services, sectors, reasons), so every concept had the same page structure.

This prompt names that family and rules it out. It assigns three directions that are far from it and far from each other. It also allows honest photography, a free palette and a free page structure.

## How to run it

1. Create **three new Stitch projects**, one per direction. Do not generate all three in one project: Stitch carries one design system across a project's screens, and that pulls the concepts together.
2. Upload only the logo (`logo-wordmark.png`). Do not upload earlier screenshots, design files or a design system from the old projects.
3. In each project, paste the **shared brief** and then **one** direction block (A, B or C).
4. Ask for desktop (1440 px) and phone (390 px) versions of the homepage. A phone screen with the menu open is useful too.
5. If a result drifts back to paper, drawings or black-and-yellow, reply with: "This is the excluded family. Start again from the direction block."

## Shared brief

```text
Design the homepage for ketztek GmbH, a young process-automation and software firm in Winterthur, Switzerland.

THIS IS A FRESH START
More than ten earlier concepts were turned down because they were too similar to each other. They all came from one family of ideas. Do not use anything from it:
- paper, documents, forms, work reports, certificates, checklists, shift boards, tags, title blocks, drawing sheets, blueprints, floor plans, route maps, dimension lines, technical-drawing figures, "Fig. 1" captions, sheet or drawing numbers
- a white, off-white or light-grey page with black text and thin rules; hairline "Swiss grid" layouts; editorial, newspaper or monograph layouts
- black and yellow as the colour scheme of the page; yellow bands, yellow highlighter marks, black hero bands, diagonal stripes taken from the logo
- before-and-after diagrams of a process
- a condensed or expanded grotesque headline face as the main personality
If a concept could be described with any of the words above, discard it and start again.

WHO IT IS FOR
Plant, site and operations managers at small and mid-sized industrial and construction companies in German-speaking Switzerland. They are practical, sceptical of consultants and allergic to hype. They read on a phone between site visits and decide on a desktop.

WHAT "PROFESSIONAL" MEANS IN THIS MARKET
- Understatement and precision. No superlatives and no exclamation marks. Concrete units ("half an hour", "two weeks") instead of big promises.
- Trust comes from real things: real people, real places, plain facts. The large Swiss industrial firms show their people at work and their facts. They do not decorate.
- A calm, unpushy offer. One clear next step, never a pop-up or a countdown.
- German is the first language. The English copy below will be translated and runs about 30 percent longer. Leave room for it. Swiss German conventions apply: "ss", not "ß"; «guillemets»; dates as 21.11.2026.
- Header with a "DE | EN" switch. Footer with Impressum and Privacy links.

WHAT COMPETITORS LOOK LIKE (stand apart from all of it)
Navy, bright blue, teal, neon green on dark, or orange accents. Inter, Manrope, Montserrat, Satoshi or Clash Display type. Dashboard mock-ups, laptop and phone mock-ups, stock photos of handshakes and people pointing at screens, gradient blobs, rows of client logos.

WHAT ketztek DOES
Process analysis, automation on Microsoft 365 and the Power Platform, and custom software for industrial and construction companies. Its promise is honest sizing: "Sometimes the honest answer is two weeks of work, and we'll tell you that." The people who analyse the process are the same people who build the fix, and they come on site.

HONESTY RULES
The firm is young. It has no client logos, reviews, case studies, certifications, figures or founding year. Do not show or imply any of them. Invent no numbers, names, quotes or team members. Any photograph must be a clearly labelled placeholder for a photo ketztek will shoot itself. Write the shot under the placeholder in one line, for example "Photo to shoot: ketztek engineer and site foreman at a container table, Winterthur". No stock-style images.

BRAND
The uploaded logo is the black "ketztek" wordmark with a yellow double z. Use it as supplied, once in the header, on a light area. Never recolour or redraw it. On a dark or coloured area, show a white-letter version labelled "proposed logo variant". The yellow #ebc34d and the black belong to the logo. Choose the rest of the palette freely, as long as it sits well next to the logo. Yellow may appear elsewhere only as one small accent, or not at all.

ACCESSIBILITY FLOOR
WCAG 2.2 AA contrast. Body text at least 17 px on phones and 18 px on desktop. Tap targets at least 44 x 44 px. Visible keyboard focus. No horizontal scrolling from 320 to 1440 px. One orchestrated motion moment at most, switched off under reduced motion. No cookie banner.

THE PAGE MUST
- Say on the first screen what ketztek does, for whom, and where.
- Show the primary action "Book a free 30-minute call" on the first phone screen without scrolling. Secondary action: "Send a message".
- Cover, in any order and form you choose: how they work (Map, Propose, Build, Teach), the five services, the two sectors, why work with them, a closing invitation and the footer.
- Look made in 2026 by a serious, modern firm that is confident enough to be small.

COPY (use these lines as written wherever you use them; choose the order, grouping and form yourself; add no new claims)
- H1: "Find the process that costs you time, and fix it with the tools you already have." A concept may set a shorter line from this list above or beside it as a display line.
- Supporting line: "Analysis, automation, and custom software for industrial and construction companies. Based in Winterthur, working across Switzerland."
- "Most inefficiency isn't a software problem. It's the recurring and repetitive tasks piling up in the background."
- How we work: Map ("We start by tracking the root of the problem."); Propose ("Then we propose how to solve it. Often the solution is smaller, faster, and cheaper than you expected."); Build ("The people who map your process are the people who build the fix."); Teach ("Then we teach them to run it.")
- Services: Process Analysis & Optimization ("We map your workflows end to end, work out where the time and money are going, and hand you a prioritized plan you can act on."); Automation & Digital Workflows ("Many bottlenecks disappear in weeks rather than months, using licences you already pay for."); Custom Software Engineering ("When standard tools reach their limit, we build."); Prototyping & Feasibility ("Before you commit a budget, find out whether the idea holds."); Workshops & Training ("We teach your team to get real value out of Microsoft 365 and the Power Platform.")
- Sectors: Industry & Manufacturing ("Machine data nobody looks at. Quality reports assembled by hand. Maintenance that runs on experience because the records are incomplete. We connect what's already there and digitize what's still on paper."); Construction ("Site documentation spread across paper, Excel and messaging apps. Defect lists that never quite match the plan. We move site reporting onto something people will actually use with gloves on.")
- Why ketztek: "We size the solution to the problem" ("Sometimes the honest answer is two weeks of work, and we'll tell you that."); "We work alongside you, not for you"; "A small team with a deep bench"; "Analysis and delivery from one team"; "You keep what we build"; "Swiss-based, on site when it matters" ("Process work can't be done over video calls alone. We come to the factory and to the building site.")
- Closing: "Not sure where to start?" ("Most of our projects begin with one frustrating process. Tell us about yours. In half an hour, we'll give you an honest opinion on whether it's worth fixing and roughly what it would take, before any invoice exists.")
- Footer: ketztek GmbH, Schulergasse 9, 8406 Winterthur, Switzerland; info@ketztek.ch; Impressum; Privacy.

AVOID THESE GENERATED-DESIGN HABITS
All-caps labels above every heading, strings joined with middle dots, numbers on things that are not a sequence, rows of identical rounded cards with soft shadows, a fade-in on every section, an arrow appended to every link, a cream background with a terracotta accent, and a near-black background with one neon accent.

RETURN
- A name and a one-sentence idea.
- One sentence on why it suits a Swiss plant or site manager.
- Palette with hex values, typefaces (avoid the competitors' faces above) and the layout idea.
- The one signature element and where it appears.
- The first screen at 390 x 844 and 1440 x 900, then the full homepage at 390 px and 1440 px.
- One line: which excluded idea is closest to this concept, and how it differs.
```

## Direction blocks

Paste exactly one of these under the shared brief.

### A. People and places

```text
DIRECTION A: PEOPLE AND PLACES
The promise is personal: the people who map the process build the fix, and they come to the factory and the building site. Make the page about that. Lead with large, art-directed photography placeholders of real working moments (each labelled with the shot to take): a ketztek engineer with a foreman at a site table, a hand on a machine panel, a tablet used with work gloves, the Winterthur office street. Photography fills the width; text sits in generous, calm space beside or over it with enough contrast.
Choose a warm, confident page colour, not white and not grey, and a typeface with a human voice. A serif or a humanist sans is welcome. The feeling: a capable neighbour, not an agency.
```

### B. The tool itself

```text
DIRECTION B: THE TOOL ITSELF
ketztek builds working tools, so the homepage behaves like one. The first screen is a small instrument: the visitor picks a sector (Industry & Manufacturing, Construction) and the task that costs them time (quality reports, maintenance records, defect lists, site reporting, approvals by email). The page answers in plain words drawn only from the copy: what ketztek would look at first, which service fits, and the button "Book a free 30-minute call". No dashboards, charts, invented data or fake software screens. The interface is the design: clear controls, satisfying states and one confident colour.
Choose a palette that feels like a well-made physical control panel or a modern piece of equipment, not a SaaS product. Show the instrument in its default state and in one answered state, on phone and desktop.
```

### C. Colour and type

```text
DIRECTION C: COLOUR AND TYPE
Take the Swiss poster tradition, not the Swiss grid. Each section is one bold statement on a full-width field of strong, unexpected colour, set in very large type. Use two or three colours that no competitor uses and that are not black and yellow. Shapes, if any, are big, simple and abstract, and not taken from the logo. The page should be memorable at a glance and still calm to read: one idea per screen, plenty of space, body text at normal reading size.
Choose a distinctive display typeface (not a condensed or expanded grotesque) paired with a quiet text face.
```

### Optional fourth: material and light

```text
DIRECTION D: MATERIAL AND LIGHT
Swiss precision manufacturing, shown as material. Use large macro photography placeholders of surfaces from ketztek's clients' worlds (machined steel, poured concrete, timber formwork, a cable tray), each labelled with the shot to take. Use a muted, premium palette drawn from those materials, a refined typeface, and a lot of space. The feeling: quiet quality, like a Swiss toolmaker's catalogue, never luxury.
```

## Changes from the first Stitch prompt

- Names the excluded family (paper, documents, drawings, black-and-yellow, hairline grid) and tells Stitch to start again if a concept falls into it.
- Runs one direction per Stitch project, so no shared design system pulls the concepts together.
- Assigns three directions outside that family: photography and people, an interactive tool, and colour-field poster type. A fourth is optional.
- Frees the palette. Yellow and black stay in the logo only, and a white-letter logo variant may sit on dark or coloured areas, labelled as proposed.
- Allows photography as labelled placeholders for ketztek's own shoot. Stock images stay banned.
- Frees the page structure and order. The copy stays verbatim and claims stay limited to what ketztek has said.
- Keeps the local-market rules: German first, Swiss spelling and formats, DE | EN switch, Impressum and Privacy, understatement, no invented proof, the competitors to stand apart from, and the same accessibility floor.
