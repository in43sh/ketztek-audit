## 1. Role and goal

You are the design lead of a studio that gives every client a visual identity nobody mistakes for anyone else's. The client has seen four options and found them correct but bleak. Your job is one new option ("E") that:

- looks **solid and professional** to a plant manager or a construction company's operations lead in Switzerland;
- looks **modern**, made in 2026, without chasing trends;
- **stands out** among the Swiss automation and Power Platform consultancies listed in section 5.2;
- keeps everything that already works: the offer, audience and booking button on the first phone screen, honest copy, accessibility.

You may keep, rework or drop any idea from options A to D, including the "work report" signature, if you can say why.

## 2. What the four options taught us

Assessment of the screenshots (Home page, clean view, 1440 px full page and 390 px first screen):

| Option | Best at | Weakest at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, calm | Clearest offer: what, for whom, where, and "Book a free 30-minute call" on the first phone screen. The work-report block is a real idea from the visitors' world. Honest copy. | One 620 px column leaves half the desktop empty. Same visual weight from top to bottom. The grey page (`#eeeeeb`) reads dull. No imagery. Reads like a document, not a company. |
| B, structured | Uses the desktop width: steps across the page, headings in a side column, reasons in three columns. The before/after line drawing of a quality report is the only original visual and the best one. A large quote gives the page rhythm. | Still grey and flat. Wide bold type on every heading feels heavy. "01 · Label" numbering on sections that are not a sequence, and middle-dot labels, are tells of generated design. The quote repeats copy from the list below it. |
| C, bold | Memorable. The logo's double stroke drawn large is a strong brand device. | Black and yellow at full strength read as warning signage on a building site: aggressive for a consultancy. Long text in white on black is tiring. Trust drops. |
| D, balanced | Best of the four: the double stroke as a restrained accent behind the work report, a white band to separate the problem section, phone layout unchanged. | Still bleak: large grey areas, little colour, one illustration, the hero is text plus a form-like box, most sections are lists of text, a thin footer. |

**Keep:** the logo's black and yellow as the identity; the double stroke as a subtle brand device; the technical line-drawing idea; the first-screen booking button; a strong left-aligned grid; the honest, plain copy; the accessibility floor.

**Drop or rethink:** grey as the page colour; one wide bold weight carrying every heading; white "sheet" boxes everywhere (form-like and dated); numbering on non-sequences; duplicated lines; a single text column on desktop; yellow used at full strength over large areas.

**Missing:** a visual anchor on the first screen; typographic refinement (weights, sizes and spacing that feel precise); a colour system with a calm base and one or two supporting tones; visuals that show what ketztek actually builds; credible trust signals without invented content; a footer with presence; small, purposeful interaction details.

## 3. Fixed constraints

- **Logo:** use logo option B ("Balanced") from `client/logo-options/`: the light version on light backgrounds, the dark version (white letters) on dark backgrounds, and the icon for the favicon and touch icon. Copy the SVGs into the prototype's `assets/`; never recolour, redraw or restyle them further. B is a proposal ketztek has not approved yet, so mark it once on the page with `[[draft: proposed logo refinement, needs approval]]`, and make sure the design still works with the original PNG logo on a light background: if ketztek keeps the original, only the light header and footer can be used.
- **Brand colours:** black `#000000` and the logo yellow `#ebc34d`. Any other colour you add must work beside them and must not be the category default (5 of 7 competitors use navy, blue, teal or green).
- **Content:** use the copy bank from the earlier design prompt. New wording is marked `[[draft]]`; German you write is `[[draft, native review]]`; anything only ketztek can give is `[[supply: ...]]`. Never invent clients, figures, quotes, logos, certifications, prices or years.
- **Images:** no stock photographs presented as ketztek's work, no device mock-ups. The design must look finished without photos. Optional photo slots are allowed if they are clearly marked `[[supply: own photo ...]]` and the layout works when they are empty.
- **Fonts:** self-hosted WOFF2 only, never loaded from Google or another font service at run time. Free (SIL OFL) by default; a commercial Swiss typeface is allowed as a recommendation only, with its licence cost stated and a free fallback actually used in the prototype.

- **Stack:** static HTML and CSS generated by `build.py`, plus at most a little vanilla JavaScript for the phone menu and the form. No framework, no build pipeline, no tracking.
- **Research:** passive only. Read `robots.txt` first and keep any crawl delay (ketztek.com and ioz.ch ask for 10 s). Never submit forms, book, or contact anyone. Raw captures and screenshots of other sites stay outside every repository (use the session scratchpad).

## 5. Research

Keep it focused: about a third of the session. Record every finding with its source URL and the date read.

### 5.1 Local visual culture

1. **Swiss design heritage, applied today.** How the Swiss Style (grid, asymmetric layouts, flush-left type, strong hierarchy, restrained colour) shows up in current Swiss corporate and B2B sites, and where it has become a cliché.
2. **Award winners.** Recent Best of Swiss Web winners and shortlists ([bestofswissweb.ch](https://www.bestofswissweb.ch/)) in B2B, technology and industry categories, and Swiss Design Awards where relevant. Pick five that look professional and modern; note one principle to borrow from each, not a look to copy.
3. **Swiss industrial and construction brands.** For example Sulzer, Rieter, Bühler, Stadler, Burckhardt Compression, Schindler, Hilti, Implenia. These are the companies ketztek's visitors work for or alongside. Note how they signal precision and trust: type, colour, imagery, density, diagrams.
4. **Local conventions** the design must respect: Swiss Standard German (no "ß"), «guillemets» in German quotes, phone format `+41 52 ...`, dates like `21.11.2026`, the Impressum and privacy pages (Swiss nDSG), German first or English first for this audience, and how sites present the language switch.
5. **Type.** Which typefaces Swiss B2B sites use; the Swiss foundries' typical faces (for example Grilli Type, Dinamo, Swiss Typefaces, Optimo) and their licence costs; free OFL alternatives with similar qualities. Avoid what the competitors use (Manrope, Montserrat, Inter, Satoshi, Clash Display, Maven Pro, Trade Gothic) and what the current site uses (Inter, Instrument Sans).

### 5.2 Competitors, re-read

Re-read the first screens at 390 and 1440 px of [evolyze](https://www.evolyze.ch/), [Theia Solutions](https://theiasolutions.ch/), [PowerAlps](https://www.poweralps.ch/), [Stellar Automation](https://www.stellar-automation.ch/), [KMUpower](https://kmupower.com/), [IOZ](https://www.ioz.ch/) and [Noser Engineering](https://www.noser.com/), plus three to five more Microsoft Power Platform or process-automation firms in the Zurich and Winterthur area that you find through search. For each: what makes it look professional or not, and its accent colour and first-screen type. End with the gap ketztek can own.

### 5.3 Reference board

Eight to twelve references, Swiss or international, of B2B, engineering or industrial-services sites that are professional and modern. For each: the URL, one sentence on the principle to borrow, and one on what not to copy. Include at least two that show complex technical work through diagrams, interface fragments or drawings rather than photos.

### 5.4 Research output

`ketztek-audit/internal/research/design-v2/ketztek-visual-research.md`: sections 5.1 to 5.3, then a one-paragraph synthesis: what "professional and modern" means for this audience in Switzerland, in concrete visual terms.

## 6. Directions and decision

1. Load the `frontend-design:frontend-design` skill before designing and follow it.
2. Draft **three directions** that differ in more than colour. For each: a name; the idea in one sentence; palette (4 to 6 named hex values with roles, including black and `#ebc34d`); type (families, weights, scale for phone and desktop); layout concept with ASCII wireframes of the Home first screen at 390 and 1440 px; the visual system (illustration, diagrams, interface fragments or photo slots); the one signature element; at most one motion moment. Possible territories, not prescriptions: a Swiss-grid system with a technical-drawing illustration set; a product-led direction showing the tools ketztek builds (an inspection checklist, an approval flow, a maintenance log) as crisp interface fragments; a refined version of the work-report idea with a new visual layer.
3. **Check each direction** against the skill's list of generated-design tells and against the category defaults in 5.2. Revise anything that would fit any competitor, and say what you changed.
4. **Score** A, B, C, D and the three new directions from 1 to 5 on: looks professional and trustworthy; looks modern; stands out among the competitors; fits the Swiss market; makes the offer and the booking clear on a phone; looks finished without photos; accessible; buildable as a static site. Pick the direction that scores highest; it must beat D on at least six of the eight. Record the table and the reasons.
5. Write the chosen direction as `ketztek-audit/internal/research/design-v2/ketztek-design-v2-spec.md`: tokens (colour, type, space, radius, rules), components and their states, the illustration rules, page-by-page section plans, and what must not drift.

## 7. Build

1. In `ketztek-demo`, create a new branch `design/option-e` from `main`. Keep options A to D intact on `main`.
2. Rework `build.py`, `css/style.css` and, if needed, `js/site.js`. All colours, sizes and spaces as CSS custom properties. Keep the draft and supply notes and the `?clean` view.
3. Build in this order, with a checkpoint after each:
   - **Checkpoint 1:** the token sheet and the Home first screen at 1440 and 390 px. Take screenshots, critique them against sections 2 and 6, fix, and **stop to show the user before going on.**
   - **Checkpoint 2:** the full Home page at both widths.
   - **Checkpoint 3:** the Automation page, Contact (with the form and its error state), the phone menu, the 404 page, then the remaining pages in the same system.
4. Visuals: build them in SVG and CSS (drawings, diagrams, interface fragments). Keep line weights, corner radii and label styles consistent across all of them, so they read as one set.
5. Use Playwright for screenshots in the session scratchpad. Look at each screenshot before moving on: a picture is worth more than a description.

## 8. Checks (once, at the end)

Run the quality floor from section 3 once on the finished pages, at 320, 360, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px: first screens, contrast of every text pairing, tap targets, line length, focus order and visibility, sideways scrolling, reduced motion, the form flow (empty submit, a wrong email, a corrected submit with one click), and that every link works. Serve the site with `python3 -m http.server` for the checks, since the 404 page uses root paths. Fix what fails and re-check only what you changed. Optionally run `frontend-design-audit:evaluate` on the result as a final usability read.
